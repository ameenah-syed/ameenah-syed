I’m a computer science student drawn to the places where computation meets the living world: signals from the brain, models of learning, and the mathematics underneath complex behavior. I’m currently building my foundations across computer science, statistics, mathematics, and neuroscience, and putting them to work through research and hands-on projects.

My long term goal is doctoral study in a field that lets me investigate these connections deeply. I’m especially interested in **applied mathematics and logic**, **computational neuroscience**, and **network theory**—areas that offer different ways to study structure, inference, and emergence. I’m still exploring where those paths meet most productively, and I want my work to help make that meeting concrete.

## What I’m working on

- **EEG preprocessing** — contributing to a pipeline for preparing neural signals for analysis through my work with Cincy Brain Lab.
- **ICVision** — working on a computer-vision classifier in the lab, building experience with the full path from data to model behavior.
- **Brain–computer interface research** — interested in how entrainment and neural dynamics can inform more capable, responsive interfaces.
- **Ask a Grad Student** — developing the idea for a platform where students can ask graduate researchers questions through live or text conversations. The goal is to make academic knowledge more reachable and human.

## Questions that keep me curious

- How can mathematical structure help us interpret noisy biological signals?
- What can network models reveal about brains, learning, and collective behavior?
- How do logic and uncertainty shape the way intelligent systems reason?
- How can research tools make complicated ideas easier to explore and share?

## Things I’m building my toolkit in

`Python` · `C++` · `Machine Learning` · `Statistics` · `Linear Algebra` · `Signal Processing` · `Computer Vision` · `Neuroscience`

I care about understanding the fundamentals, not just getting a model to run. I like tracing an idea from its mathematical assumptions to its implementation—and then asking what the results actually mean.

## A direction, not a shortcut

I’m working toward a career that keeps research, engineering, and meaningful human questions in the same room. A PhD is part of that ambition: not as a title to collect, but as the training to pursue difficult questions with rigor and contribute something useful to how we understand complex systems.

If you’re working at the intersection of mathematics, machine learning, neuroscience, or networks, I’d be glad to learn about what you’re exploring.
